# Design plan

Written by the build agent before the theme CSS.

## Where the direction comes from
A joiner's workshop has two marks of its own: the steel rule, and the pencil line on timber with its cut mark.
The site uses both. A rule with millimetre ticks runs down the left edge of every screen. The four steps of a
job sit on a dimension line, the way a joiner marks a length on a drawing: two end stops, the span between.

## Colour
| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Plaster | `#F3F1EC` | background |
| Bog oak | `#26201B` | text, buttons |
| Pencil red | `#A8321C` | the cut marks, the dimension line, the lead-time figure |
| Shaving | `#D8D0C2` | hairlines |

## Type
- Barlow Condensed 600, in capitals, for the name and headings: the stencilled lettering of a workshop sign.
- Source Serif 4 for reading.

## Principles
1. One memorable thing: the rule down the left edge. Everything else is quiet.
2. No photographs: the client has none of their own and refused stock. Type and line carry the page.
3. The lead time is the one number, set large: it is what a buyer of joinery asks first.

## Review against the generic defaults
- First idea: a warm wood-tone background. Dropped: it reads as a template for "craft". Plaster white instead,
  with the timber named, not pictured.
- First layout for the products: three cards. Dropped for three ruled rows, name left, sentence right.
